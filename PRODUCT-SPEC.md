# The Signal — Product Specification

*A complete breakdown of what The Signal is, what it does, how it's built, and how it's used.*

Version of skill at time of writing: **8.33.0**. This document is descriptive (it explains the system as it stands); the authoritative editorial source is `.claude/skills/the-signal/references/editorial-spec.md` and the operational source is `.claude/skills/the-signal/SKILL.md`.

---

## 1. What The Signal Is (in one paragraph)

The Signal is a **personal weekly magazine for a single reader**, generated by AI and published as a website. Every Sunday morning it produces one polished, image-rich HTML "issue" — designed to be read on a tablet, with coffee, in 30–45 minutes of selective reading from a 60–90 minute magazine. It is **not a news digest or an RSS feed**. It is a curated Sunday supplement: this week's news across the reader's interests, plus evergreen features, recommendations, fun facts, and reference data, all written in a confident magazine voice. It is produced entirely by a multi-agent Claude Code pipeline (the `the-signal` skill), published to GitHub, auto-deployed by Cloudflare, and delivered to the reader as an installable progressive web app (PWA) with offline support and push notifications.

## 2. Purpose & Design Philosophy

### The problem it solves
The reader already gets headlines from BBC News. What he doesn't get is **analysis, context, the stories behind the stories, and curated discovery** — all assembled in one beautiful artifact, on his schedule, tuned to his range of interests without being a firehose. The Signal is the "perfectly curated Flipboard + great Sunday supplement + weekly planner" rolled into one.

### Core principles
- **A magazine, not a feed.** Editorial judgement, a point of view, visual craft. No infinite scroll, no volume-for-volume's-sake.
- **One reader, invisible.** The reader's profile drives *what* is researched and selected, but is **completely invisible in the prose** — every section is written as if for 100,000 readers (the "Cardinal Rule"). No "perfect for your son," no second-person address.
- **Borrowed angles, our voice.** The magazine takes confident, opinionated positions — but the opinion is always a real take that *actually exists in the sources*, voiced as the magazine's own. It never invents a thesis from nothing.
- **Ship every Sunday, no matter what.** A broken-but-imperfect issue beats no issue. The pipeline self-heals where it can and always publishes; defects are tracked afterward, never block the reader's Sunday read.
- **Lens, not filter.** The reader profile narrows *angle*, never *appetite* — a genuinely big world story gets covered even if it's off-profile, and every issue carries a floor of genuine discovery (≥3 "you wouldn't have looked for this yourself" items).

## 3. The Reader (the audience profile)

A single, specific reader profile drives all selection:

- **Who:** Tech-literate professional in Northern Ireland with a 10-year-old son. Does **not** want work/enterprise content. Reads on a **Xiaomi Pad 8 tablet** (~800px portrait — a key layout constraint).
- **Wants:** Analysis and context beyond the headlines he already has.
- **Interests:** World news & geopolitics; gaming (Nintendo/Switch 2, Steam Deck, GeForce Now, tablet games); consumer tech (Pixel, Xiaomi, e-readers); consumer AI tools; LEGO; football (Juventus/Serie A + Premier League + Champions League); golf (majors/Ryder Cup); film/TV/streaming; Star Wars; fantasy/sci-fi books (Malazan, Cosmere — **never any spoilers**); synthwave/retrowave; fitness (structured gym training, recreational running with a 10k target, kettlebells, mobility/recovery, Garmin/training science); podcasts & audio dramas; light NI local; Disney Parks/Efteling; meal prep & high-protein cooking; home-gym building; Android/tablet productivity; digital-product entrepreneurship (Etsy/Notion/Kindle Scribe templates); UK personal finance & fintech (Monzo/Revolut/Starling); European family travel.
- **Hard exclusions:** Work/enterprise content, celebrity culture, royal family, generic fitness advice, AI-generated images, fabricated links. **UK/national politics is out by default** (in only on a genuine landscape shift, e.g. a government actually falling).

The profile is stored and evolved in `state/signal-state.json` (interests, current reading/watching, training phase, upcoming trips, etc.).

## 4. Issue Formats

The Signal runs **one pipeline** for every issue. The format only changes *which* chapters get written and *how* writers are sequenced — not which pipeline phases run. There are eleven formats: one standard, ten specials.

| Format | Length | Trigger | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard Weekly** | No hard target — a healthy Sunday read sized to the week's news (~20–30 pages) | Default, every Sunday | The full edition: World, gaming, football, culture, fitness, history, recommendations, release radar, and more, each as Lead + Catch-Up. |
| **Deep Dive** | 8,000–12,000 words (flex to 20–25k) | Manual, or quarterly auto from a backlog | Single-topic exploration with a committed editorial frame (The Argument) and cross-media pointers (Keep Digging). |
| **The Countdown** | 5,000–7,000 words | Manual / auto ~2–3 weeks before a trip | Pre-event hype build-up. "Hype over homework" — ranked attractions, accommodation, mood board, moments worth the trip. |
| **The Field Guide** | 6,000–10,000 words | Manual / auto ~6 weeks before a trip | **Food guide** for a trip — source-led Unmissables + ranked meal-slot coverage. The agent "has no taste buds": every verdict traces to a real source. |
| **The Versus** | 5,000–7,000 words | Manual ("A vs B") | Head-to-head comparison. Picks a winner (or a conditional one); a holiday subtype runs round-by-round with a mandatory "price-trap" Value round. |
| **The Rewind** | 8,000–12,000 words | Manual, or half-year (last Sun of June) / year-end (last Sun of Dec) | Panoramic retrospective across *all* interests, organised around a single named Throughline + a Memory Test (Will Stick / Might Stick / Will Fade). |
| **The Season Review** | 7,000–10,000 words | Manual / auto when a league season concludes | End-of-season retrospective for one concluded subject, anchored by hedge-free Scorecards. |
| **The Starter Kit** | 4,000–6,000 words | Manual ("getting into X") | Zero-to-competent beginner's guide, anchored by a concrete day-by-day One-Week Plan. |
| **The Shortlist** | 3,500–5,500 words | Manual | Tiered, opinionated picks (Top / Strong / Wildcards), bookended by The Lens (criteria) + The Cheat Sheet (summary table). |
| **The Next** | 3,500–5,500 words | Manual only ("after I finished X") | Post-completion recommendations anchored to a named "Itch," each pick with a Start/Reassess/Then On-Ramp. |
| **The Lookahead** | 4,500–6,500 words | Manual only | Multi-event preview of a window (~6–8 weeks); every calendar item gets a verdict tag (🔥 Don't miss / 👀 Worth a look / ⏳ Wait for reviews / 🚫 Probably skip). |

**Holiday formats** (Countdown, Field Guide) have their own distinct visual identity (the `.hol-*` component layer with motion); the other specials share a literary "magazine-spread" design system; the weekly uses its own section-component vocabulary.

## 5. Editorial System (how issues are written)

### The Standard Weekly's structure
The weekly is built from a fixed roster of sections (each appearing once) plus 2–3 rotating slots:

- **Fixed sections:** The World This Week · Pixel & Byte (gaming + LEGO) · The Toolkit (tech/AI/apps — yields when thin) · The Touchline (football/sport) · Screen & Sound (film/TV/music) · The Release Radar (mandatory: 15–20 upcoming releases across ≥4 media categories) · The Session (fitness/training) · On the Radar (events grid).
- **Rotating sections** (selected by cadence, most overdue first): The Shelf · This Week in History · The Listening · The Money · The Places. **The Saga** runs only when a peg fires it.
- **Sub-formats:** Screen & Sound runs a "Director's Cut" essay monthly; History runs "A Closer Look" deep dive every ~6 weeks.

### The Lead + Catch-Up shape
Every fixed section is built on this shape:
- **Catch-Up roundup (the spine):** the week's real domain news the reader would otherwise have to trawl for — each item a *specific* fact (name, number, dated event) + why it matters + a link. Plus one-line safety-net headlines so no big story is ever dropped.
- **Lead (optional, earned):** an angled centrepiece. It earns its slot only if (1) the story actually *moved* this week and (2) the magazine can add a real, sourced angle beyond the headline. Inventing an angle to fill the slot is banned. A section may run pure Catch-Up, or yield entirely when the week is thin.
- **Companion (optional):** a second deep piece on a different topic family.

### Cardinal editorial rules
- **Reader-profile invisibility** (Gate 1A) — the single most-enforced rule.
- **No spoilers, ever** — absolute but never announced.
- **7-day freshness** — news must be from this week; evergreen features must be clearly framed as features.
- **Facts trace to the research bundle** — the writer states only facts the researcher gathered; a stated result must have *actually happened* by the run date. Fabricated scorelines, made-up podcast summaries, etc. are unforgivable errors.
- **Every substantial item links out** to its specific source.
- **Images mandatory, never AI-generated, always credited**, sourced diversely (no single domain >50%, Wikimedia capped).
- **Topic lock** — a story can't dominate the Lead rotation (sliding-window frequency cap) and can't re-lead on a holding pattern without a datable new development.
- **No single theme owns an issue** — enforced by a theme-clustering check.
- **Plain English** — prose must communicate, not perform; an extensive catalog of banned "tropes" (lit-review walls, vignette-template openings, hollow connective sentences, hedged answers, etc.) is enforced by an honest read at Phase 7.

### Visual / markup discipline
- **Closed markup contracts** — components must use exact canonical tag+class combinations; invented markup misses the CSS and breaks styling. Enforced by grep gates.
- **Ground discipline & accent lockdown** (specials) — chapters own their background ("ground"); coral accent is reserved for three things only; components lock their own text colour for readability.
- **Stat budget** — hard caps on stat blocks per issue so numbers don't flatten into noise.
- **Tablet-first** — the Xiaomi Pad 8 (~800px) sits between breakpoints, so layouts are always sanity-checked at tablet width; covers fill the full viewport (`100dvh`).

## 6. The Generation Pipeline

The Signal is produced by a **multi-subagent pipeline** orchestrated by the `the-signal` skill, running on Claude Code (manually or on a schedule). The orchestrator dispatches specialized subagents (researcher, planner, writers, repair, scorer) and runs a series of deterministic script "gates" between phases.

### Models
The orchestrator **must** run on an Opus 1M-context model at or above the 4.7 floor (4.8 preferred) — verified at "Step Zero" before anything else, because a smaller model overflows context during writer fan-out. All subagent roles default to Opus 4.8, with Sonnet 4.6 as a rate-limit/availability fallback. Genuinely mechanical work (stitching, validation, image substitution) is done by deterministic scripts with no model at all.

### The phases
| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Step Zero** | Verify the orchestrator model meets the floor; abort if not. |
| **Phase 0** | Clone repo, read state, **decide the format** via calendar/event/cadence trigger logic (P1 calendar → P2 event → P3 cadence safety net → else weekly). |
| **Phase 3** | Derive execution mode (parallel vs sequential writers); spawn the **researcher** subagent to gather facts, images, and release-radar items into `research-bundle.json`. |
| **Phase 3a-verify / 3b** | Orchestrator independently WebFetches every image URL; the **research-bundle validator** enforces image-source diversity and the structured fact-provenance rules. |
| **Phase 4** | Spawn the **planner** subagent → `chapter-plan.json`; validate it (`validate-chapter-plan.py`). |
| **Phase 5** | Spawn **writer** subagents (one per chapter), parallel or sequential, each writing chapter-only HTML. |
| **Phase 6** | **Stitch** — deterministically concatenate chapters, wrap in scaffold, inject CSS/JS, run vocabulary/activation gates. |
| **Phase 7 → 7.8** | A battery of gates: per-chapter compliance grep, topic-lock, theme-clustering, the plain-English read, release-date sanity, structural+asset validator, image-source diversity, prose-rhythm (literary specials), and a no-network DOM "visual smoke test." |
| **Phase 8** | Stitched-issue cross-chapter gate (consistency, link health, editorial/visual quality). |
| **Phase 9** | **Self-healing repair** (max 3 rounds): programmatic image auto-repair first, then targeted repair subagents for content defects. |
| **Phase 9.5** | **Editorial quality scoring** — a dedicated scorer agent grades the post-repair artifact on five dimensions (voice, density, structure, opening, throughline). Observational only — never blocks publish. |
| **Phase 10** | **Always publishes.** Update state, mirror images locally, generate cover thumbnail, promote the issue to the archive hero slot, push to GitHub `main`. Cloudflare auto-deploys; a GitHub Action fires push notifications. |

### Gate discipline
Every script-backed gate is run **by the orchestrator itself** and judged by its **exit code** — a subagent claiming "gate passed" is never acceptable evidence. This rule exists because subagents have been observed reporting success for gates they never ran.

### Why so many gates? (history baked in)
The pipeline's complexity is a direct record of real failures: fabricated image URLs, special-edition markup leaking into weeklies, results asserted before they happened, UK politics dominating three weeks running, a Deep Dive that performed essayism without teaching. Each gate maps to a specific shipped (or nearly-shipped) defect. The governing **meta-rule is "adjust, don't accrete"** — when an issue disappoints, the fix is to sharpen an existing principle/gate, not bolt on a new script.

## 7. Observability: Cost & Quality Logs

Two append-only logs in `state/` make the pipeline measurable:

- **`cost-log.jsonl`** — one line per subagent call (role, model, issue, chapter, retry, outcome). Answers *"what did this issue cost?"* and validates whether the model fallback chains are sized right. Reviewed with `cost-summary.sh`.
- **`quality-log.jsonl`** — one line per issue from the Phase 9.5 scorer (five dimension scores + overall + weakest + a note), stamped with both `writer_model` and `scorer_model`. Answers the question the cost log structurally cannot: *"was the issue any good?"* — and, over time, *"did the stronger writer model actually score higher?"* Surfaced publicly on a baked `quality.html` page linked from the archive, and in the terminal via `quality-summary.sh`. Kept honest by a periodic human-anchor score.

## 8. Technical Architecture

### Output
Each issue is a **single self-contained HTML file** (`issues/signal_<format>_<date>.html`) with all CSS and JS injected inline. Issues range ~100KB–700KB. The live archive (`index.html`) lists every issue with a hero slot for the newest.

### Hosting & deploy
- **Source of truth:** the GitHub repo `stevenmcdowell89-hash/the-signal`. (The pipeline runs in an ephemeral container — only the repo is durable.)
- **Deploy:** Cloudflare Pages auto-deploys on every push to `main`. `_worker.js` / `wrangler.jsonc` configure the Pages project; `functions/` holds Cloudflare Pages Functions.

### Progressive Web App (PWA) + offline
- `manifest.json` + `sw.js` (service worker) make the site installable on the tablet and fully offline-capable.
- Two precache paths: **push-triggered** `deepPrecacheIssue()` (fetches the new issue HTML + every image *before* showing the notification, so the issue is offline-ready by morning even if untapped) and a **page-open** `precacheFromIndex()` safety net.
- Images are mirrored locally to `assets/cached/<hash>.<ext>` at publish time (`post-publish.sh`) so the PWA works offline and isn't dependent on external CDNs.

### Push notifications
- Web Push via VAPID. `functions/api/` provides `subscribe.js`, `notify.js`, `vapid-public-key.js`; subscriptions are stored in a Cloudflare KV namespace (`SUBSCRIPTIONS`).
- `.github/workflows/notify-on-publish.yml` fires a push on any `main` push that adds a new `issues/*.html`.
- `setup.html` generates VAPID keys in-browser; `generate-vapid.py` is the CLI alternative. Full setup is documented in `OPERATIONS.md`.

### CI validation
- `.github/workflows/issue-validation.yml` re-runs the structural and image-URL gates in an unrestricted-network environment (the pipeline's sandbox can't reach arbitrary hosts, so its image checks degrade to advisories). On defects it auto-files a tracking GitHub issue — informational, not blocking, unless branch protection is enabled.

## 9. State Management

`state/signal-state.json` is the single durable memory between runs. It tracks:

- **Issue numbering** — standard weeklies are sequentially numbered (`last_issue_number`); **specials are never numbered** (referenced by format + topic).
- **Recency tracking** — `topics_covered_recently`, `section_topics_recently`, rotating-section `last_appeared` cadences, `recent_facts`, `recent_next_week_themes`, `recent_special_formats`.
- **Ongoing stories** — per-topic `lead_history` (drives the topic-lock cap), `aliases`, and `last_development` (drives the "did it actually move?" test).
- **Trips** — `upcoming_trips` with legs, `access_constraints` (e.g. car-free), and which trip-special is due. Drives Field Guide / Countdown auto-triggers.
- **Deep Dive schedule** — `deep_dive_schedule` (quarterly cadence) + `deep_dive_backlog` (topic queue).
- **Personal context** — `currently_reading`, `currently_watching`, `training_phase` (block boundaries).

State is read at the start of every run and written at the end, committed to the repo alongside the issue.

## 11. How It's Used

### Manual generation
Start a Claude Code session in the repo and invoke the skill: type `/the-signal`, or say *"Run The Signal"* / *"generate this week's Signal"*. For a special: *"Run a Deep Dive on [topic]"*, *"Run a Versus — [A] vs [B]"*, *"Run a Field Guide — [trip]"*, *"Run a Next — after [the thing I finished]"*, etc. The pipeline runs end-to-end, unattended-capable, and reports the published URL on completion.

### Scheduled generation (Claude Code on the Web)
A Sunday-morning trigger (e.g. cron `0 6 * * 0`) fires a fresh session against `main` with the canonical prompt (in `OPERATIONS.md`). A SessionStart hook installs Python deps; the pipeline runs through all phases, picks a special edition automatically if a calendar condition is met (else a standard weekly), and publishes to GitHub. Cloudflare auto-deploys; subscribed devices get a push with the issue pre-cached for offline reading.

### Reading
The reader opens the live site (installed as a PWA on the tablet), reads the newest issue from the hero slot, and browses past issues from the archive grid. With push enabled, the issue is delivered and cached overnight, ready offline by Sunday morning.

### Editing the system (not generating)
To tweak styling or structure, edit the relevant file directly (the Asset Map in `SKILL.md` points to the exact CSS/template file). CSS is split across `assets/css/` in **alphabetical cascade order** — never reorder/rename; a build script concatenates them. To change editorial substance, edit `references/editorial-spec.md` and re-run `scripts/slice-spec.sh` to refresh the per-role sliced spec.

## 12. Operating Principles to Remember

1. **The deliverable is the published URL** + a record of any residual defects — not a perfect issue.
2. **Always publish.** Self-heal within budget; ship the best-effort issue; track the rest in CI. Last week's issue stays live so the site never lacks content.
3. **The repo is the only durable store.** Everything worth keeping is committed and pushed.
4. **Compliance ≠ quality.** Gates prove an issue is *shippable*; the quality log is the only instrument that measures whether it's *good*.
5. **Adjust, don't accrete.** When an issue disappoints, sharpen an existing rule — don't add another script.
