A week of handovers.

Power changes hands in Westminster, a trophy in New Jersey, the grid at Spa — and the two that matter most are still being decided as the coffee cools.

Two landscapes shifted on the same Sunday, and I couldn't pull them apart. This morning Britain has a Prime Minister-in-waiting who reached No.10 without a public vote: Andy Burnham, declared Labour leader on Friday, sworn in tomorrow. This afternoon in New Jersey the largest World Cup ever ends — 48 teams, 104 matches, 39 days — with Spain and Argentina, the two best-ranked sides on the planet, kicking off as this issue is read.

The honest thing is that both stories are unfinished at the moment they reach the page. I can tell you what a mid-term handover of power means, and what the first 48-team final settles about the tournament's shape; I can't tell you who lifts the trophy, or what Burnham does with a job no election gave him. A week of transitions, then, caught mid-motion — Westminster, MetLife, and a Formula 1 grid reshuffled overnight at Spa. The outcomes are still ahead of us. What they mean, we can start on now.

— The Editor
