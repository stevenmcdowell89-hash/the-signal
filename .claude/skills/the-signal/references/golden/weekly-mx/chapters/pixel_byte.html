Two beloved back catalogues, rebuilt inside a month — July is usually the quiet quarter of the gaming calendar, the lull before the autumn stampede, and this one runs true to form. What fills the gap is a remake summer: two of the medium's most-loved games returned to the workbench and sent back out within weeks of each other, one already here and one about to land. If the new-release slate is thin, the reissue slate is doing the heavy lifting.

FIG.Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced — the 2013 pirate adventure rebuilt from the ground up, out since 9 July.Ubisoft (via Xbox Wire)
  • 108.6

    Black Flag, resynced

    Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced (9 July, PS5/Xbox/PC) is a ground-up remake of 2013's much-loved pirate adventure — the golden-age-of-piracy entry that a lot of players still name as the best the series ever managed. The pull was never really the assassinating; it was the ship. Black Flag was the one where the sailing and the sea shanties outshone the stealth, where a full open ocean sat beneath the map and there was always a horizon worth chasing. Rebuilding it for current hardware is a low-risk proposition, precisely because the thing being rebuilt already worked. If you missed it the first time round, or simply fancy the crossing again, this is the version to board. Set sail again.

  • 111.3

    Halo comes to PlayStation

    Halo: Campaign Evolved is the other half of the season, and the more surprising one. It launches 28 July on Xbox, PC (Steam) and — for the first time for a mainline Halo remake — PlayStation 5, arriving day one on Game Pass, with early access from 23 July for anyone who pre-orders. Frame it however you like: a Halo game on a Sony console is the sort of sentence that would have read as a typo not so long ago, and it is the clearest sign yet of how far the old platform walls have come down. The campaign itself is the reason the series mattered in the first place; putting it in front of an audience that has never been able to play it is the interesting part. Still forthcoming as this goes out. 28 July, everywhere.

  • 113.9

    An indie-led lull

    Between the two big reissues, the week itself (13–19 July) was quiet and indie-led — a scattering of roguelites and co-op horror rather than anything marquee. That is not a complaint. A week with nothing you have to buy is a week to clear the backlog: finish the thing you started in spring, or dip into a small release you would otherwise have skipped straight past. Treat it as the catch-up window it plainly is. Backlog weather.
