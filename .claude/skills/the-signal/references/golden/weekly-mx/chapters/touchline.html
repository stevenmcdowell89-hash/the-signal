The week sport handed its trophies over — and the two biggest are still being decided as this is read. There is a bluntness to sport I find restful on a Sunday: a tennis final ends when the last ball drops, a race ends at the line, and no verdict sits waiting on a committee. This week alone crowned two champions on the grass at Wimbledon and settled a ten-goal bronze match — yet the two prizes that matter most, a World Cup and a Grand Prix, are both still to come. As I write, neither has a winner. By tonight, both will.

101.2 · WORLD CUP · THE FINAL

A final still to be played

Spain against Argentina at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, kicking off at 15:00 Eastern today — the last act of the largest tournament football has staged, the first with 48 teams and 104 matches, run across the United States, Canada and Mexico since 11 June. It is also the first final to bring together the world's top two ranked sides, Argentina first and Spain second, and the first all-Spanish-speaking final since 1930. Neither has lifted anything yet: as this goes out the match is still to be played, and I'm not about to guess it. The path there is settled, though. Spain saw off France 2-0 in the semis; Argentina edged England 2-1. And the bronze is already banked — England beat France 6-4 on Saturday in a ten-goal thriller that a third-place playoff had no business being. Kick-off pending.

    Two Wimbledon crowns, one of them kept

    Jannik Sinner beat Alexander Zverev 6-7(7-9), 7-6(7-2), 6-3, 6-4 to defend his title over three hours and forty-six minutes — a fifth Grand Slam, and only the tenth man in the Open Era to retain the gentlemen's crown. He dropped just three sets across the fortnight; the one he lost in the final was the only real wobble. The day before, Linda Nosková came through an all-Czech women's final over Karolína Muchová 6-2, 5-7, 6-3 to take a first major and become the youngest champion since Petra Kvitová in 2011. 2 champions.

  • 104.7

    Spa sets a grid, saves the race for later

    The Belgian Grand Prix runs this afternoon, and the front of the grid has a surprise on it. Kimi Antonelli took pole at Spa with a 1:44.361, edging Max Verstappen into second. Lando Norris set the third-fastest time in qualifying but starts 13th after a ten-place grid penalty, which promotes George Russell up the order. Whether Antonelli can convert a maiden pole into something more, and how far Norris can climb back through the field, is the whole story of a race that hasn't happened yet. Lights out later.

    Serie A draws its map for August

    With the international season closing, the domestic one is already sketched: the 2026-27 Serie A fixtures are out, the campaign opening the weekend of 22-23 August with Torino hosting AC Milan. Juventus begin away at newly promoted Frosinone — a gentle-looking start on paper, though those rarely stay gentle. Kick-off 22 Aug.
