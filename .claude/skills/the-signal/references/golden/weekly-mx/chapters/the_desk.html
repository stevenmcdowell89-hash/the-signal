The Session

FITNESS · BLOCK 3, CONCURRENT LOAD

The 10k is going back into a week that's built for building muscle, and the old worry surfaces with it: does running kill your gains? For a recreational lifter, the honest answer is very little. The meta-analyses on concurrent training are consistent — adding endurance work does not significantly blunt hypertrophy, and it trims maximal strength by only about 5–10%. Power and explosive output take the biggest hit; the muscle-in-the-mirror job you're actually running Block 3 for is barely touched. So land that plainly before the caveats: a few runs a week will not undo a hypertrophy block.

Now the qualifiers, because they're where the free wins are. The interference effect scales with endurance volume — a couple of moderate runs is noise, marathon-week mileage is not — so keep the running honest and moderate while the block is on. Separate the two disciplines by 6–8 hours where the day allows it, so fatigue from one doesn't tax the other. If they have to share a session, lift first, run second — you protect the quality of the loaded work that drives the adaptation. And because running's eccentric, pounding load lands hardest on the legs you're also trying to grow, some of those runs can be swapped for cycling when lower-body hypertrophy is the week's priority: similar aerobic stimulus, far less muscle-damaging impact.

DO THIS WEEK · THE SESSION

Put the hardest lower-body session and the longest run on different days — or at least 6+ hours apart — and when they do collide, barbell before the road.

The interference is small and mostly hits power, not size; spacing the two and lifting first keeps even that small cost off the work that grows muscle.

The UK mortgage price war is deepening even though the Bank of England hasn't moved. The base rate sits at 3.75%, and markets expect a hold when the Monetary Policy Committee meets on 30 July — yet lenders aren't waiting for the Bank's cue. Nationwide has cut its rates roughly four times in a month, Yorkshire Building Society twice in a single week, and the average two-year fix fell 0.16 percentage points in June, the fastest monthly drop since October 2024. The important read: the cheaper deals landing right now are coming from lender competition, not from a rate cut. Fixed mortgages are getting cheaper while the base rate stands still.

That distinction matters for timing. Because the discounting is a scramble for market share rather than a response to falling base rates, there's no single announcement to wait for — the good deal is the one on the table today, and a better one may or may not follow. The useful mechanic is that most lenders let you lock a rate up to six months ahead of your current deal ending, and swap onto a cheaper one if rates fall further before you complete. You get the downside protection of a secured rate with the upside of the ongoing price war, which is about as close to a free option as this market offers.

DO THIS WEEK · THE LEDGER

If a fix expires within about six months, get a new deal quoted and locked now — most can be secured up to six months ahead and swapped if rates drop before completion.

The cuts are coming from lenders competing, not the Bank easing, so there's no rate decision to wait on. Locking now protects you and still lets you trade down if the price war keeps running.
