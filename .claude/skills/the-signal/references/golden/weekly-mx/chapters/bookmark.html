• Not with a Bang

    Science fiction · new July 2026 · literary

    Temi Oh's newest, out this month, drops a family into an apocalypse — but the catch is that each of them lives with a different mental-health condition, and the paranoia turns out to be more than symptom. It's the kind of current, character-first SF that uses the end of the world to look hard at how the mind copes when the ground gives way. The premise keeps the reader guessing about what's real and what's illness, and it does so without letting the human story slide out of focus. Fresh off the press and worth getting to before the spoilers land — the sort of literary SF that rewards reading before the discourse catches up.

  • Alien Clay

    Science fiction · alien world · prison colony

    A penal colony marooned on a genuinely strange alien world, from a writer at the top of the modern SF field. Adrian Tchaikovsky builds ecosystems that feel thought-through rather than decorative, and this one pairs that world-building with a political thriller's edge. The setup — dissidents shipped off-world and put to hard labour under an authoritarian regime — gives the alien biology real stakes rather than set dressing. If it slipped past you when it landed, it's the ideal reset between doorstop fantasy series — self-contained, sharp, and unmistakably its own thing.

  • Some Desperate Glory

    Science fiction · space opera · Hugo winner

    Emily Tesh's Hugo-winning debut follows a soldier raised inside a militarised, radicalised world and traces what it takes to see past it. It's taut and driven entirely by character — the space-opera scaffolding is real, but the book's engine is one person's slow, painful reckoning. A lean, propulsive read that earns its award shelf and rewards a single-weekend sitting.
