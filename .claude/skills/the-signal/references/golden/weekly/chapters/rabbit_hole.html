TUMBLE INTO Telstar, the No.1 that outlived it

Within weeks of Telstar 1 going up, the eccentric British producer Joe Meek released an instrumental called "Telstar" — credited to The Tornados — built to sound like a satellite singing. It became the first record by a British group to top the US Billboard Hot 100. He made it not in a plush studio but in a rented flat above a leather-goods shop on Holloway Road, layering tape loops, spring reverb, distortion and a clavioline, an early electronic keyboard. He was a bedroom producer decades before the term existed. Follow his Wikipedia entry and the thread runs straight through the clavioline and the Mellotron, the BBC Radiophonic Workshop, Delia Derbyshire and the Doctor Who theme, the early Moog — right down to the neon-and-Carpenter synthwave still being made today.
