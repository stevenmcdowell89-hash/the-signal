A week of signals received.

On a satellite that sang, a Cup played out an ocean away, and the quiet work of tuning back in.

Every story that mattered this week arrived from somewhere else. I went looking for a thread and kept pulling the same one: things reaching us across distance, faint or loud, and asking to be received. Sixty-four years ago this week a grapefruit-sized sphere called Telstar rose off Cape Canaveral and, for a few minutes each orbit, taught two continents to watch each other live. It lasted seven months. The habit it started never switched off — and it's the Long Read, because it's the whole magazine in miniature: a signal sent, tuned in, kept.

The rest of the week rhymed with it. Jannik Sinner held Centre Court to keep his Wimbledon crown; a World Cup narrowed to a heavyweight last four, with England and Argentina meeting on Wednesday night. The harder transmissions came from the Gulf, where a June ceasefire was declared over and the mediators started shuttling again. And closer in: a week home from Efteling, the marathon block folded away for good, the first proper session of Block 3 logged — the small, unglamorous work of tuning back in to ordinary life. Pour the coffee. This one's built for a single sitting.

— The Editor
