    Project Hail Mary lands on Prime

    The Andy Weir adaptation — directed by Phil Lord and Christopher Miller, with Ryan Gosling as amnesiac science teacher Ryland Grace — reached Prime Video on 3 July, just 105 days after its cinema run. It is the same brand of optimistic, problem-solving hard SF that made The Martian such good company: one person, a long way from home, working the problem with a pencil and first principles. The reviews backed it up — a 94% critics' score and a box-office haul north of $680m, which made it one of the biggest films of the year. Much of the warmth was aimed at Rocky, the alien Grace meets out there, brought to life on set by puppeteer James Ortiz; the film's best trick is turning a first-contact story into a two-hander about friendship across an impossible gap, proof, as one critic put it, that Gosling "can have chemistry with a rock." Not every notice was a rave — one or two found it a touch familiar — but for a Sunday night it is exactly the sort of hopeful, competent science-fiction that sends you to bed cheerful. Sunday-evening sci-fi.

    A melodic-synthwave supergroup moment

    Michael Oakley's "Falling Skyward" (NewRetroWave, 10 July) gathers three of the scene's strongest voices into one track: co-written with Ollie Wride, with backing vocals from Dana Jean Phoenix. It opens on arpeggiated, crystalline synths over a club-ready beat before the vocals lift it skyward, exactly the widescreen, heart-on-sleeve register the genre does best. It lands off the back of Oakley's well-received album Prologue from earlier in the summer, a run of form that has him spoken of among the scene's best right now. What sets it apart from the usual all-synth fare is the live playing underneath — layered electric and 12-string guitars give it real body and stop it feeling like a period pastiche. Put it on for the last few kilometres of a long run and it does the job it was built for: chorus arriving right on cue, everything reaching upward. It is only a two-track release, but on this evidence Oakley is writing some of the most confident melodic synthwave going, the kind that sounds enormous in headphones and somehow bigger still in a car at night. Long-run fuel.
