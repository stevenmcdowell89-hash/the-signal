A signal from across an ocean, settled the hard way — while the week's other verdicts stayed stuck in committee, sport kept doing what it does: deciding things on the scoreboard, in front of a crowd, with no room for a recount. There is a bluntness to it that I find restful on a Sunday. A tennis final ends when the last ball drops; a knockout tie ends when the whistle goes; a bike race ends at the line, and the standings are the standings. This week gave us two fresh Slam winners at Wimbledon, a men's World Cup narrowed to a heavyweight last four, and the yellow jersey pausing to catch its breath high in the Massif Central before the Alps arrive.

98.4 · WIMBLEDON · THE FINAL

Sinner keeps the crown

Jannik Sinner beat Alexander Zverev 6-7(7), 7-6(2), 6-3, 6-4 to defend his title — a fifth Grand Slam, and only the tenth man in the Open Era to retain the gentlemen's crown. It was not a rout. Zverev took a tight opening set on the tie-break and pushed the second to the same place before Sinner steadied, reeled off the breaker, and never trailed again; from two sets to one up the outcome felt settled long before it was. Zverev handed him the compliment straight afterwards: "He's still the best player in the world, I do believe that… probably three guys who can challenge him." Sinner returned it, calling Zverev's game one that's "growing and growing." The day before, Linda Nosková came through an all-Czech women's final over Karolína Muchová 6-2, 5-7, 6-3 — a first Slam for a player who had never previously gone past a major quarter-final. Two champions in two days, one of them defending, one of them arriving. SW19, retained.

    The World Cup, down to a heavyweight last four

    France, Spain, England and Argentina came through the quarters — Bellingham with a brace in extra time past Norway, Julián Álvarez settling Switzerland at 112'. Not one of the four semi-finalists is a surprise, which is its own kind of drama: the seeds have held, and now the giants have to play each other. The semis are set — France v Spain in Dallas on Tuesday, England v Argentina in Atlanta on Wednesday, the tie of the round — before Sunday's final. Tuchel stayed pointedly unimpressed even in victory: "not happy with the performance… sloppy," if "proud" of the result. Both matches are still UPCOMING as this goes out. 4 left.

  • 104.7

    The Tour reaches its first rest day

    Mathieu van der Poel took stage nine into Ussel on Saturday, a rider of the classics reminding the peloton he can still win from a break in July. But the general classification reads exactly as billed: Tadej Pogačar in yellow, 2:42 up on Jonas Vingegaard, as Monday's rest brings the first pause of the race. It is a healthy lead rather than a decisive one — the high mountains, where Vingegaard has hurt him before, are still to come. +2:42.

  • 106.0

    Juventus work the window

    Turin made the Jeff Ekhator loan permanent, buying the young Italian striker from Genoa outright for a reported €16m or so — the club backing a forward it already knows rather than gambling on a name. Meanwhile the pursuit of PSG's Randal Kolo Muani rolls on, a costlier and less certain proposition, with the market open until the end of the month. One deal banked, one still in the air. 1 in, 1 pending.
