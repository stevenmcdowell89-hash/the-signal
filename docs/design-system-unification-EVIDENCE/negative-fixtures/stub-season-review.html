The Season, In Brief

The season began under grey skies and ended in a heat that nobody had planned for. Across thirty-eight weeks the table shifted in ways that felt both inevitable and strange. Form came and went, and the middle of the table stayed as crowded as it had ever been. Every result seemed to matter and then, a week later, seemed to matter a little less.

The early promise of autumn gave way to a long, grinding winter of narrow margins. By the turn of the year the pattern had set, though few were willing to say so aloud. Injuries reshaped ambitions, and the depth of a squad became the quiet story of spring. There were nights when the football was thrilling and nights when it was merely dutiful.

The numbers told one story and the eye told another, and both were partly right. A run of four wins can hide a great deal, and for a while it hid almost everything. Momentum is a word people use when they cannot quite explain what they are seeing. The bottom of the table was decided later than anyone expected and earlier than it felt.

Possession rose and fell without ever settling into anything that resembled a lesson. The final weeks arrived with the usual mix of arithmetic, hope, and quiet dread. What looked like a crisis in November read, by May, as an ordinary dip in a long year. The league is less a single narrative than a stack of small ones piled loosely together.

Each club carried its own weather, and the season was the sum of all those local skies. A late equaliser in a forgotten fixture can bend a table more than any grand result. The margins were thin enough that a single deflection felt like it decided a campaign. By the end the standings looked tidy, which is the one thing a season never truly is.
